Built around how Ohio eats

Star ratings won't tell you where to get a five-way after midnight, or which pizzeria still cuts its pie in squares. Ohio Eats is organized the way people here eat: Cincinnati chili, Ohio's pizza styles, Amish Country cooking, Cleveland's pierogi and corned beef, Lake Erie perch, Barberton chicken and Toledo's Hungarian hot dogs, each place checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source. In Cincinnati and Columbus you see the City's own inspection record, never a grade from us, and Apple Maps' live card supplies ratings, hours and photos.

Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out on a back road.

What stays on your phone

Ohio Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is Ohio Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The Cincinnati chili, Ohio pizza, Amish Country and other food lists are our own research from fall 2026: a page that names each place for the food (a local news story, Ohio Magazine, a tourism board or the place's own site), and something from 2025 or 2026 that shows it's open. The wider directory of 25,356 restaurants starts from Overture Maps' open place data, keeps the kinds of listing that proved reliable against Cincinnati's and Columbus's own license and permit lists, and adds licensed restaurants from those lists.

Where can I get Cincinnati chili?

Our chili parlor list has 6 hand-checked places. In the web search, the Cincinnati chili list puts the nearest first once you share your location.

How do I check an Ohio restaurant's inspection?

Ohio has no statewide grade. The Cincinnati Health Department publishes its inspection results, and for Cincinnati places the app and this site show the City's own result for the latest routine inspection, word for word, with the number of violations cited. Columbus Public Health publishes each food permit's status, which we show for Columbus and Worthington places. Most other health districts offer search pages on their own sites.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
