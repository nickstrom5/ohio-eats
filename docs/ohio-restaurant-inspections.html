Cincinnati and Columbus restaurant inspections, explained

Ohio has no statewide restaurant grade and no statewide inspection data. Local health districts license and inspect restaurants under the Ohio Uniform Food Safety Code, and two cities publish the results in bulk: the Cincinnati Health Department and Columbus Public Health. Here is what each one publishes, and where the 3,312 places in our data stood.

What each city publishes

We show each city's own words and compute no grade of our own. Neither city's data marks a violation as critical or not, so we don't either.

  • Cincinnati

    Since the City's 2024 records system, every visit carries the City's own result. We show the result of each place's latest routine inspection, word for word, with the number of violations cited at that visit and its date: 860 places, with visits through October 14, 2026. "Violations Abated" means a follow-up visit found earlier violations fixed.

    • Approved - No Violations 135 places
    • Approved - Minor Violations 604 places
    • Approved - Violations Abated 103 places
    • Not In Compliance 18 places

    The City's records.

  • Columbus

    Columbus Public Health, which also covers Worthington, publishes each food permit's current status. It publishes no inspection dates or violation counts in bulk, so for 2,452 Columbus-area places we show the status alone, as of October 24, 2026.

    • Standards Met 2,448 places
    • Under Enforcement 3 places
    • Closed for Violation 1 place

    The City's records.

One inspection is a snapshot of one day, and a violation corrected on the spot still counts. We don't list places by their results here; the web search can sort the Cincinnati list by the latest result.

The rest of Ohio

Elsewhere, Ohio's health districts publish inspection reports one search at a time on their own sites, or not online at all: Hamilton County outside Cincinnati, Franklin County outside Columbus, Dayton and Montgomery County, Summit and Lucas counties offer search pages, and Cleveland's results aren't online. Those results aren't here. Look a place up in the Ohio Eats web app for its city license or permit and, in Cincinnati, its latest result and recent visits.
